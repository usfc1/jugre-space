# Mission Designer and Crew Life Support

Mission timestamps, dated planetary planning and the short-flight format use the existing flight state and HUD. See [short flights, resource time and scale](SHORT_FLIGHTS.md).

## Standard Mode

The default mission authoring entry is a five-step Standard flow: destination, compatible objective, automatic or crewed profile, payload, and preparation. The **Engineering** tab uses the same visual layout with five freely accessible steps: mission, orbit, payload, carrier, and review. All manual assembly controls, project library, import/export and detailed reports are retained. The Russian interface uses native DOM, the existing Three.js loader, bundled planet textures and spacecraft/rocket assets; no remote assets or services are required. Both modes share one preview canvas and renderer without changing the builder or flight camera.

`source/standard-mission.mjs` is an intent-to-spec adapter for `readMission`, `planMission`, `assessVehicle` and `searchVehicles`. It creates the same canonical mission schema used by Engineering, project records and flight checkpoints. An AI Creator can supply that schema through `readMission` without introducing another simulator. Standard tests alternative panels, batteries and attitude hardware against existing resource checks, then selects an eligible carrier using the existing search and mass ordering. The final report uses the selected candidate's actual calculated values, including UNKNOWN structural analysis. No new physical solver or fabricated prices are used.

`source/standard-mission-view.mjs` owns navigation, draft storage (`jugre.standard-mission.v1`), payload search, selection, budget correction and overview. Launch saves through the same project library instance as Engineering, calls the existing launch preparation and starts the normal flight entry point. `source/mission-preview.mjs` renders cached thumbnails and interactive models. Instrument renders are schematic illustrations of the existing fictional catalog. `source/standard-mission.css` is embedded by the normal offline build.

Supported intents are Earth orbit, satellite deployment, Earth science, the existing four-person lunar orbital expedition with return, and uncrewed flyby/orbiter missions to Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune. Earth science defaults to four hours and a 12 MB downlink goal. Different crew sizes, an automatic lunar expedition, planetary landings in newly designed missions, crewed interplanetary profiles and docking remain unavailable. Lunar and interplanetary cargo choices affect mass; individual experiments on that cargo are not simulated. Full launch and expedition prices remain unavailable; satellite costs are sums of the existing educational component prices. The lunar ship and entry limits remain educational. Switching to Engineering exposes the shared specification for detailed changes; Standard reconstructs supported high-level choices using its automatic profile.

Node tests cover state validation, schema compatibility, real pointing and resource assessment, unaffordable configurations, correction and lunar resources. Browser tests cover all five steps, reload persistence, Engineering interoperability, budget correction, Earth launch, lunar configuration and mobile navigation. This update targets the browser build; the portable Windows EXE remains unchanged.

## Interplanetary missions

Both authoring modes support an automatic probe flyby or orbital insertion at Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune. Start from Earth/LC-39A, choose the destination and objective, choose additional cargo, then prepare the mission. Engineering exposes the probe's dry mass, propellant and specific impulse on **Нагрузка → Межпланетный модуль**. The orbit fields describe the target planet's orbit or closest-approach altitude. A blank transfer time searches feasible durations around the nominal half-ellipse estimate; a manual duration must lie between 50% and 150% of that estimate. A UTC epoch fixes departure; otherwise a synodic window is searched from the recorded reference epoch. The total-duration input, if supplied, must allow transfer, destination operations and launch reserve.

The existing physical launcher first achieves the measured Earth parking orbit. The probe then separates and spends its own finite propellant on an impulsive departure. `mission-planets.mjs` computes a zero-revolution solar Lambert transfer using the existing universal-variable Kepler propagator and approximate dated JPL planetary elements. Cruise position and velocity use the same propagated state. This remains a two-body solar transfer with patched-conic departure/capture budgets and instantaneous burns, without N-body gravity or gravity assists. At an orbital destination, actual planet-relative state vectors must have negative energy and matching apsides. Probe fuel, chosen departure epoch, transfer duration and parking departure geometry are saved and restored. Short flights map ordinary gameplay steps to reduced distances and the corresponding mission-clock epochs. Resources advance by gameplay seconds in this format.

The fictional JUGRE probe defaults to 450 kg dry mass, 12,000 kg propellant (24,000 kg for the Mercury starter) and 450 s specific impulse. Additional cargo is added to its inert mass, and the full probe mass is included in launcher selection. These are game hardware inputs, not ratings of Voyager or another real spacecraft. The Voyager mesh is a schematic visual stand-in. Mercury, Venus, Uranus and Neptune have procedural schematic maps; Mars, Jupiter and Saturn reuse the existing maps. All assets remain offline. Existing FlightSystems accounts for finite battery and reference generation/load during coast. Radiation, detailed thermal qualification, instrument experiments, downlink, planetary landing, crew and Earth return remain unavailable in these profiles. Historical solar routes use approximate reconstructions with documented event times.

Planet sizes/distances are from the [NASA NSSDCA fact sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/index.html); solar GM, AU and planetary-system GMs are from [JPL astrodynamic parameters / DE440](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/astro_par.html). `interplanetary-flight.mjs` owns phase burns and flight sampling. The optional `interplanetary` specification field preserves compatibility with older schema-1 projects. Unit tests fly all fourteen destination/objective combinations, check solar energy, arrival endpoints, fuel, bound orbits and checkpoint restoration. Browser tests cover all planet previews, engineering fuel failure, Mars flyby and Neptune orbit through the normal launch/save/load flow.

## Earth observation and project library

Engineering shows one group of fields at a time, a live mission summary, and a planet preview that changes to the selected rocket after calculation. Optional descriptions, launch conditions, carrier limits and detailed calculations are collapsed. The footer keeps navigation, saving and the current primary action visible on desktop and mobile. Edits invalidate the previous configuration and require recalculation before launch. `source/engineering-mission-view.mjs` manages this presentation and navigation; `source/mission-designer-view.mjs` continues to own validation, search, persistence and launch preparation. No mission schema or physics changes are needed.

In **Проектирование миссии → Engineering**, select **Наблюдение Земли**. The starter design uses a wide-angle camera and a four-hour mission deadline. Edit the orbit, instruments, power, storage, radio and attitude hardware, then select **Рассчитать** and inspect a carrier's checks. The default picks the smallest eligible educational payload allowance. In **Спутник · научные операции**, monitor generation, load, battery, memory and station contacts. The initial queue contains one observation per selected instrument. Use the flight controls to change the science and radio switches or enqueue another operation. The default plan requires orbital insertion, separation, observation and delivery of 12 MB by the deadline.

In **Мои проекты** on the **Миссия** step, open, duplicate, archive, restore, delete, import and export projects. Edit the name in the mission form and save it to rename. The comparison table supports two to four projects and switches between design values and the last matching finished flight. Save and select a carrier to compare launch mass and ideal Δv. The library stores configurations, selected assembly or carrier and a terminal result summary. The existing flight checkpoint continues to store the exact flight, including spacecraft state. A JSON project transfers the configuration and any recorded summary. Import validates schema, catalog IDs and mount topology before writing. Legacy single-mission records are read through the existing schema migration and copied to the library on the first library write.

Select **Двухступенчатый спутник** to load an editable assembly using **ИС-125**. The example sets a 70% thrust limit for the upper stage and a 200 × 310 km target. To build another axial stack, attach an ИС-125 or ИС-250 sleeve above the lower stage, fit the next tank in the sleeve's forward mount, and install its engine in the separate engine socket inside the sleeve. Each stage has an isolated tank inventory. The sleeve drops with the lower stage. The upper stage starts after the ignition delay and the drag model uses the diameter of the remaining stack. The engineering report lists stages and flags missing tanks or engines, incompatible alignment, branching stage topology and payload or control fitted to a discarded stage.

Flight checkpoints retain exact physical and operational state. Diagnostic trajectory history is resampled to at most 1,024 rows in the checkpoint, retaining the earliest and latest available samples. The current session still keeps up to 7,200 rows for graphing and export.

All new hardware costs, performance and ground station ratings are educational values. A historical stack's modeled payload mass serves as the teaching scenario's replacement allowance. The launch simulation still determines whether it actually delivers the required orbit. The visual uses the existing Mayak model. Separation transfers the measured orbital state without a modeled ejection impulse. Two-body propagation and Earth rotation drive station geometry. The Sun has a fixed inertial direction at launch and the Earth uses cylindrical shadow. A nominal solar pointing factor of 0.65 and 92% charging and discharging efficiencies are used. Link speed is capped by radio and station ratings, scaled by elevation and distance. Contact forecasts sample geometry every 30 seconds and omit future outages and energy availability. One instrument or transmitter operates at a time. The chart keeps up to 120 samples of battery energy and electrical load. Orbital observations currently include daylight, ground-footprint and local sensing modes. Surface sampling, celestial pointing, failure probabilities, thermal engineering, development schedules and comprehensive mission economics remain open.

The test suite checks the circular-orbit geometry, eclipse energy balance, data conservation and capacity, partition-independent resource stepping, validated project round trips, physical insertion and a power-deficit case. Browser scenarios exercise Earth observation, staged launch, four-person lunar flight and three-project comparison and sharing. Arbitrary radial staging and multiplayer remain open.

## Four-person lunar orbital expedition

Select **Лунная экспедиция**, then **Рассчитать**. The template supplies four crew members, 232 kg of cargo, a 120 km circular lunar orbit and a four-seat educational spacecraft. Choose Saturn V or SLS, then prepare and launch. The **Экспедиционный корабль** and **Профиль жизнеобеспечения** panels expose service propellant, orbital work and return duration, consumables, equipment and power. The flight supports pause, checkpoint restoration and up to ×20,000 at isolated coast. Use the crew panel to assign rest and activity or operate life-support equipment. The flight log records the three impulses, measured lunar apsides, entry angle and splashdown speed.

The simulator derives the parking plane and departure window from the actual launch state. It projects the reference Moon into that plane and solves two-body Earth and Moon legs joined at the lunar sphere of influence. A supplied UTC epoch selects the existing approximate lunar position at arrival; undated profiles retain the reference geometry. A reversed patched-conic solve targets a −5.9° entry at 120 km, and the lunar orbit coasts to the computed return window after the requested operations time. The TLI impulse spends the actual remaining upper-stage fuel before stage release. Capture and return use service propellant, and the service dry mass and remaining propellant are dropped at entry. The educational flight uses impulsive maneuvers and a coplanar circular lunar orbit; it does not integrate a moving Moon throughout every leg. Guidance in the executable profile is automatic after Earth ascent. The launch review offers full and short flights with different distance scales.

The physical entry uses spherical gravity, rotating atmosphere, point-mass drag and approximate banked lift. It integrates stagnation-point convective heat, peak acceleration and a gradual parachute deployment. The game’s teaching limits are 300 MJ/m², 13 g and 13 m/s at water contact. The splashdown model assumes an ocean recovery area. It omits geography constraints, skip guidance, ablator chemistry and radiative heating. Ship ratings and recovery limits are fictional. The ship reuses the Orion visual model. The four crew draw from the existing finite cabin model. Power generation pauses during launches, entry and modeled eclipses. Medical and radiation simulation, EVA, lunar landing and alternate recovery or abort trajectories remain open. ## Repository audit and implementation map

- JUGRE Assembly Hangar is the playable JavaScript ES module application. Three.js renders the builder, launch complex, and flight views. esbuild embeds the application, GLBs, audio, and WASM into `index.html`. The Electron project packages that HTML for Windows. The other project directories supply source models and manifests.
- `assembly.mjs` validates the ordered attachment tree and derives local poses from the mount catalog. `builder.mjs` persists that tree, supports undo, and creates the launch snapshot. The original catalog has numeric masses, engine ratings and tank capacities. It has a two-seat Kontur capsule, with oxygen endurance and battery energy ratings but without a cabin gas volume, scrubber rating, or a certified recovery configuration. The GO-34 bay fits a catalog item geometrically but lacks a published mass rating.
- `launch-dynamics.mjs` owns rotating Earth-frame ascent and parking-orbit dynamics, thrust, propellant, stage separation, drag, ground support, and ascent collision. Historical stacks have individual stage configurations. Integrated ИС-125 and ИС-250 mounts establish independent flight stages for custom axial stacks. Original demo separators and radial latches remain available as visual assembly parts.
- `flight-missions.mjs` runs preflight, countdown and fixed or bounded integration steps. `mission-timeline.mjs` consumes events and owns the displayed mission clock. Historical lunar profiles retain conics and scripted maneuver hardware; historical solar coasts now reconstruct approximate dated Lambert/Kepler paths. Entry, landing and some encounter visuals retain presentation elements. These historical profiles cannot certify a new vehicle's capture or recovery. `short-flight.mjs` defines the reduced distance scale and local-phase boundaries.
- `flight-view.mjs` initializes the bundled `physx-js-webidl` 2.9.1 WASM and steps `FlightRigidPhysics` and interior scenes for local contacts, debris, docking and loose objects. This backend is active. It does not integrate the orbital trajectory. `FlightSystems.impact` already reports hull breaches and damage. The legacy cabin pressure scalar has no gas or crew resource balance.
- The viewer uses a Russian-language DOM, built-in dialogs, one render loop, localStorage for assembly and flight, and separate pause and time-warp controls. The flight checkpoint contains the physics state, mission timeline and local rigid state. A designed flight also needs its exact assembly and objective state. The offline build is large and model-heavy; candidate generation must avoid loading or cloning meshes and should yield between bounded batches.
- The repository has Node analytical/regression tests and Playwright smoke scenarios using Edge. Validation order is mission data and engineering, real builder/flight integration, then cabin models and shared clock, followed by build and browser checks.

## Running the game

Open `assembly_hangar/index.html` in Edge or Chrome. In **Проектирование миссии**, choose **Рассчитать**, inspect the plan and candidate checks, and select **Подготовить к старту**. The default uncrewed Earth-orbit scenario can fly on a generated configuration from the existing ready-rocket catalog. Select **Текущая ручная сборка** to evaluate the vehicle on the assembly stand. Editing that assembly invalidates the prepared vehicle and the manual analysis. Applying a new generated assembly uses the builder's existing undo history.

Select **Лунная экспедиция** for the four-astronaut, 232 kg return scenario. The default configuration fills the required design inputs. The preliminary planner uses the UTC departure epoch, orbit and transfer duration to estimate lunar departure and capture.

For a two-seat engineering drill, enter the duration on **Орбита**, then crew count and individual launch mass on **Нагрузка**. Expand **Жизнеобеспечение** and provide equipment parameters. The values in the crew Playwright test are synthetic test inputs, independent of hardware ratings. An executable crewed catalog stack may still fail propulsion checks. On **Проверка**, expand **Испытание с риском** to access **Разрешить испытание с известными отказами проверок**. This keeps those checks visible and permits a flight drill on a physically representable stack. Use **Экипаж и жизнеобеспечение** during flight to switch crew activity, interrupt power or equipment, enable oxygen reserve, or set a reversible test leak. **Открыть сохранённый проектный полёт** on the pad reloads the last designed flight and its saved hardware after a page reload.

From `assembly_hangar/source`, run `npm test`, `npm run build`, `npm run test:browser`, and `npm run test:physx`. The PhysX probe rewrites its diagnostics JSON. The Electron executable and archived releases require their separate packaging workflow.

### Crew model

- The cabin starts at 101,325 Pa, 297.2 K and 1,200 Pa water vapor, with 20.95% O₂ in the dry mixture. Nitrogen represents the rest of the inert mixture. This is a standardized starting scenario assumption. Species inventories and partial pressures use the ideal gas law. Cabin gas, crew and equipment masses are added at launch. Food and water transferred into waste, condensate or crew stay aboard. Venting hydrogen and leaking gas remove mass from the launch model.
- A one-second fixed update consumes activity-specific oxygen, produces CO₂ and vapor, and consumes nominal food and water. CO₂ and vapor production are bounded by available inputs. The retained mass accumulator closes the model's mass budget. A regulator fills the oxygen deficit to the initial set point subject to delivery and inventory caps. Electrolysis uses two H₂O molecules per O₂ molecule, vents H₂, and requires at least the reversible electrical work. Water recovery transfers a user-specified fraction of processed wastewater and keeps the residue aboard.
- Battery energy is allocated to the bus and ventilation first, then scrubbing, thermal control, electrolysis and recovery. Equipment shuts off when input, supply or energy is unavailable. A rated constant generator is an explicit user input. Thermal control uses a lumped heat capacity and maximum heat rejection, with remaining rejection capacity available for condensation. The model uses sensible crew heat plus delivered electrical power as an approximate heat load. The leak is a positive pressure-differential flow approximation at the entered nominal leak rate, with no reverse inflow. Native hull impacts can set this same leak input.
- Exposure warnings compare inspired O₂ against 139 mmHg, mean CO₂ against 3 mmHg, and total pressure against 34.5–103 kPa. CO₂ averages up to the last hour using minute buckets, with a shorter history during the first hour and partial-bucket resolution. Humidity and temperature warnings use the fixed 75% and 27 °C educational thresholds. A warning affects operational readiness. Fatigue telemetry reports awake and sleep durations. Clinical diagnoses, individual injury curves, incapacitation, radiation dose and EVA physiology are unavailable.
